# Data provenance and interpretation

Primary report source: [Polizei Berlin current announcements](https://www.berlin.de/polizei/polizeimeldungen/), [2026 archive](https://www.berlin.de/polizei/polizeimeldungen/archiv/2026/). Archive list pages provide publication timestamp, title, district and article link. The adapter fetches the original incident narrative and excludes navigation and police headquarters contact details. No reliance on the intermediary site's last-50 RSS limit remains.

Keep original report bodies in local ignored SQLite only. The public code repository includes algorithms and synthetic fixtures, not a republished full-text police database. Public accessibility is not asserted to be a blanket redistribution licence. Derived publication contains short titles, IDs, classifications and original links; a deployer must check applicable reuse terms before wider redistribution.

Geometry source: [Geofabrik Berlin](https://download.geofabrik.de/europe/germany/berlin.html), OpenStreetMap under ODbL. Store download timestamp, source URL and checksum. The extract can include areas outside municipal Berlin; POIs outside the administrative boundary should not be treated as an official Berlin inventory. Basemap display and source indexing follow the supplied extract extent.

Classification is headline keyword based with an explicit unclassified bucket. POI mentions are extracted from original narrative using German patterns. Both can miss synonyms or misread context; these fields are reproducible candidate annotations, not validated event taxonomy labels.

Location rules v2 scan the whole extracted narrative, including embedded original announcements. `location_text.py` normalizes Unicode dashes, whitespace, ß and Str., and supplies bounded German adjective/genitive aliases. A prefix-factored local name matcher includes streets and named OSM parks, stations and venues. Typo suggestions go to review and never create coordinates. Generic terms such as Aufzug, Balkon and Schulweg are excluded; neighbourhood mentions cannot automatically become same-named streets. Business names require quoted/venue context and stations require station context.

Sentence rules separate the initial scene, directions, subsequent destinations, contact details and restriction endpoints. A boarding/control location can be replaced by a later explicitly described incident location. These are deterministic heuristics: a multi-incident narrative can still require review, and a mapped announcement is not a validated incident coordinate.

OSM admin-level 9/10 polygons constrain same-named geometry using the official district heading and explicit neighbourhood context. Headings such as Mitte/Neukölln do not automatically mean the smaller same-named neighbourhood. A 30 m boundary tolerance accommodates street-centre-line versus street-side administrative boundaries. These are OSM boundaries, not asserted official municipal boundaries.

Explicit house numbers can resolve against the local OSM address index (`address` precision). A named compact venue uses its real footprint/point (`place` precision), never its synthetic display circle. Same-named distant branches and places wider than 1,100 m remain under review. Single streets use a point on the indexed geometry (`street` precision); nearby same-named fragments within a 15 m buffer are clustered for display, while separated clusters and spans over 3 km remain unresolved. Parallel/fragment lengths are no longer summed as though they were one road's length. A pair is used as a junction only with explicit junction/reference syntax and a unique point or compact point cluster (≤75 m).

Every result retains a rule version, matched aliases, sentence index, optional locality, object IDs and geographic extent. Extent measures matched geometry, not a confidence interval. Address, street and place representatives remain approximate in hexes and UI. Place associations only target the explicitly matched named object, so a park centre does not darken unrelated businesses nearby. District-only reports retain no coordinates. There is no street-view inference, remote geocoder or model call.

Local comparison on 2026-09-27 used the same 1,098 article IDs/body hashes and the same verified 2026-09-25 PBF. Mapped representatives increased from 227 to 673: 496 reports gained a location and 50 previous locations were withdrawn; 29 retained locations moved more than 275 m. Of the previous unresolved groups, 26/106 name-unmatched, 129/195 long/ambiguous, 75/105 fragmented and 266/465 multiple-location reports became mappable. These are mapping coverage counts, not accuracy estimates. All reports are still retained. The publication checks confirm 673 report memberships at each hex scale and restrict place links to matched object IDs.

Spot checks included the Karl-Marx-Straße Unicode hyphens, Görlitzer Park, Stollberger Str., locality-constrained Friedrichshagener Straße, Lehrter Straße fragments, Lewishamstraße versus later closure endpoints, a bus boarding station versus the later Nieberstraße stop, and exclusion of a generic Balkon match. Recognizing a name does not ensure a usable point: Spanische Allee/Askanierring still have unresolved fragments, Märkische Allee remains too broad, and a misspelled junction/name stays in review. Raw narratives and per-report comparison artifacts remain local under `.runtime/safety/`.

## Incident-scene priority (rule version 3)

The owner requires the actual incident location to take priority over travel, escape, arrest, treatment and later road closures. Within a sentence, temporal clauses keep each named location with its own action. Explicit collision/attack/theft/damage actions and fixed physical traces such as bullet holes or graffiti outrank preparatory locations. Merely finding an injured person does not establish where the attack occurred. A real attack during a police control remains an incident scene. A short following action can refer back to a previously named scene, but cannot jump over an intervening different place.

`location_selection=first_explicit_incident_scene` selects the first explicitly linked scene in narrative order; this is a reproducible announcement-display rule, not a judgment about the most serious offence. `narrative_location` marks the less specific fallback. Excluded place roles and sentence indexes remain in `excluded_location_context`; additional distinct named scene candidates remain in `other_scene_candidates` and are shown in the browser. One announcement still contributes once to a hex: neither the source nor the count is asserted to represent one offence. Multiple roads in the same unresolved scene are not arbitrarily reduced to the first road.

An explicit junction or turning reference can involve two to four named roads. It must form a single compact common intersection (span ≤75 m). A bounded carriageway tolerance permits gaps up to 20 m, and the representative stays on the first named road. Distant intersections remain `junction_geometry_review`. Explicit named road sections use their main road between two identified boundary roads, with a metric line preserved as `reported_location_geometry`; the selected map object shows the section in purple. Hex membership uses an approximate representative on the section. Time ranges and disputes “between people” are not road sections. Missing anchors or unresolvable sections retain `street_section_review`.

Frozen-source comparison on 2026-09-27 retained all 1,098 article IDs and body hashes and reused the same OSM indexes. Of the 63 previous `multiple_locations_review` records, 34 became mappable and 29 remain under review, with separate reasons. Across all reports, mapped representatives changed from 673 to 694: 46 gained a location, 25 old locations were withdrawn under the incident-priority rules, and 11 retained locations moved more than 275 m. These are coverage/change counts, not accuracy measurements. The local comparison and full after snapshot are under `.runtime/safety/scene-priority/`.

Reviewed examples include Reinickes Hof burglary versus escape/arrest streets; Goltzstraße collision versus the later four-road closure junction; the Louis-Lewin-Straße/Annaburger Straße/Forster Straße three-road junction; Alt-Rudow/Ehrenpreisweg collision versus the incoming Neuköllner Straße; Ritterstraße assault versus later escape roads; a Wolframstraße incident section with a second accident retained as other scene candidates; and fixed bullet-hole/graffiti evidence versus an injured person merely found.

## Scene context and namespaces (rule version 4)

Station prefixes include combined S-/U-Bahn and genitive forms. An explicitly named station or park is not also treated as a same-span street. An ordinary street reference is not overridden by a distant same-named park. Generic nouns such as Innenhof, Marktplatz, Uferweg, Sporthalle and “auf der Höhe” need explicit name context; they do not automatically name unrelated OSM objects. A qualified bus-stop name such as “Street Nord” cannot silently become the street “Street”.

Short adjacent-sentence links retain “Dort”, vehicle references and repeated scene names while stopping at a different named location. Explicit junction/turn/crossing references can supply an adjacent road anchor; the existing 75 m compactness and 20 m carriageway tolerances still apply. Anchor evidence retains its original sentence index and `junction_anchor` role. Coming from a road/station remains travel origin even when another clause describes a collision. Station names following a direction are destinations. Fixed burning objects/firelight establish physical evidence; merely hearing a bang or finding an injured person does not. A dispatch station alone cannot establish a moving-train scene (`moving_scene_review`).

Neighbourhood context uses the nearest preceding explicit context consistent with the selected geometry, rather than a boarding station, later hospital or an earlier unrelated search operation. “In Mitte/Spandau/Pankow” with a same-named district heading remains district-ambiguous unless Ortsteil/Stadtteil is explicit. Official district constraints remain in force. An explicit later junction in a report labelled cross-district is not clipped to the pursuit's earlier origin. Source/locality contradictions and noncompact geometry still go to review.

The frozen v3→v4 comparison reused all 1,098 IDs/body hashes and the same OSM indexes on 2026-09-27: mapped representatives changed **694→746**, with **66 gained**, **14 withdrawn**, and **22 retained locations moving over 275 m**. **352 reports remain unlocated**. Counts measure display coverage, not location accuracy. Source checks covered all first-draft losses/large moves and the changed problematic scenes; synthetic regressions preserve the discovered failure modes. The comparison and source-specific evidence remain local under `.runtime/safety/context-v4/`.

Reviewed examples include Brunsbütteler Damm/Haberlandweg versus the Spandau district/Ortsteil ambiguity; combined station naming at Alexanderplatz; Danziger Straße/Prenzlauer Allee collision versus Greifswalder Straße origin; the Nieberstraße assault versus Adlershof boarding; Gotlindestraße/Wotanstraße collision versus sidewalk approach; Giesestraße burning objects; and Lindauer Allee damage on a bounded median section. Newly recognized scenes can still be withheld when their indexed junction/road geometry is ambiguous. All announcements remain in the publication, including unlocated records.

## Display basemaps

The standard street layer uses [OpenStreetMap standard raster tiles](https://operations.osmfoundation.org/policies/tiles/) with visible attribution, normal browser HTTP caching and viewport-only requests. There is no bulk download, offline tile prefetch or application retry loop. The provider is best-effort; a public deployment with substantial traffic needs an appropriate tile provider.

The imagery layer is **aerial orthophotography, not satellite/live imagery**: Geoportal Berlin's [TrueDOP 2026 WMS](https://gdi.berlin.de/services/wms/truedop_2026?SERVICE=WMS&REQUEST=GetCapabilities), layer `truedop_2026`. The [official announcement](https://www.berlin.de/sen/stadt/stadtdaten/geodaten-berlin/aktuelles-newsletter/) describes the March 2026 flight, 20 cm ground resolution and June publication. GetCapabilities declares [dl-de/zero-2.0](https://www.govdata.de/dl-de/zero-2-0), no access restrictions, EPSG:3857 support and Berlin bounds; CORS was checked. Browser tiles use WMS 1.3.0 GetMap in EPSG:3857, 256×256 pixels. The display source is separate from geocoding geometry and report timestamps; imagery does not validate an incident location or date.

Only one external basemap is active. Switching removes its inactive layer/source while preserving camera and all analytical layers. A tile failure stops that source and displays the local simplified road layer with an explicit failure notice. The local option needs no external basemap request, though the police/POI publication must still be available locally.

POI color denotes type. Opacity reflects the count of reports with nearby matching type; it does not score individuals, predict offending, estimate personal danger, or establish a named business caused an offence. Public police-designated kbO areas are a separate source layer. Official kbO map images are linked until verified vectors can be supplied.

Europe source catalogue: `data/safety/europe_sources.json`, with material type distinguishing designated areas, observed hotspots, and general prevention advice. General advice about pickpocketing near stations does not designate every Berlin station a crime hotspot. Check the per-country coverage ledger rather than assuming all Europe is finished.
